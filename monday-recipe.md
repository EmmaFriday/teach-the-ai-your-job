# The Monday Recipe: teach an AI your job in one lunch break

## Step 1: Pick the right task (5 min)
Choose ONE task that is: (a) something you do at least weekly, (b) annoying
but not judgment-heavy, (c) has a clear "done" you'd recognize.
Good first picks: meeting notes → minutes, weekly status report, drafting
routine replies, turning a brief into a checklist.
Bad first picks: anything political, anything with private data you can't
share with an AI, anything where being 90% right is worthless.

## Step 2: Write the skill (15 min)
Use the template (see skill-template.md). Imagine a smart new colleague on
their first day: what would you tell them about this task? Write exactly
that, in your normal language. Include your format, your tone, and at least
one "never do this" rule.
**Don't like typing? Dictate it.** Tell your AI: "I want to create a skill
that does X. It should… and it must never… Draft it and show me before
saving." Then edit its draft, the do's and don'ts still come from you.

## Step 3: Test it on something real (10 min)
Give the AI your skill plus a real (or realistic) input from last week.
Compare the output to what you actually produced by hand. Don't judge it as
"AI output." Judge it as a first draft from a new colleague.

## Step 4: Refine like a manager, not a programmer (10 min)
Where the output disappoints, don't start over. Add one rule to the skill
for each miss ("lead with the risk, don't bury it"; "keep it under 250 words")
and run it again. Two or three rounds is normal. This is feedback, the same
skill you already use with people.

## Step 5: Put it where it runs automatically (5 min)
Save the skill where your AI tool picks it up automatically (Claude skill,
ChatGPT project, saved prompt). If it lives in a document you have to
copy-paste, you'll stop using it by Thursday.

---

**The mindset shift:** you're not "prompting". You are writing down how you
work (once) and delegating. That document is yours: it works in today's
tools, it will work in next year's tools, and it gets more valuable every
time you refine it.

**Rule of thumb:** if you've explained a task to a human more than twice,
it's worth a skill.
