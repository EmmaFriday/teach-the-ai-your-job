## The idea in two sentences

Instead of asking an AI to do a task and fixing its output every time, write down
(once, in plain English) how the job should be done, the way you would brief a new
hire, and let the AI follow those instructions every time. This is called a "skill," it
requires zero code, and since December 2025 it's an open standard (Anthropic); the
same approach works in Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, or whatever AI your company uses.
(When a skill grows into a whole workflow with many outputs, I call it a playbook.
The file that generated this handout is one.)

## Why bother (the numbers)

- AI usage at work rose **13%** in 2025, while workers' confidence in using the technology **fell 18%**
  (ManpowerGroup Global Talent Barometer, ~14,000 workers; reported by Fortune,
  Jan 2026).
- For every **ten hours** AI saves someone at work, nearly **four** go back into
  correcting, clarifying or rewriting what it made (Workday, *Beyond Productivity*,
  Jan 2026, 3,200 employees who use AI at work).
- A skill is how you stop paying that tax: stop re-explaining your job to the AI
  every morning.

## Start this week: the recipe

1. **Pick your most-repeated task.** Not the hardest one, the one you'll do again
   next week (status update, meeting minutes, the same-five-questions email).
2. **Write the skill.** (Or dictate it: tell your AI what the skill
   should do and must never do, have it draft the skill, then edit.
   The guardrails still come from you.) One page, plain English, five sections:
   - *The job:* what this skill does, in one sentence.
   - *Ask first:* what the AI must know before starting (audience, deadline, inputs).
   - *The steps:* how you actually do the task, in order.
   - *What good looks like:* tone, length, format, an example if you have one.
   - *Never:* the mistakes and no-go zones (things to never claim, send, or invent).
3. **Run it on something real.** Yesterday's meeting, last week's numbers, not a
   made-up example.
4. **Fix the skill, not the output.** When something's wrong, don't edit the
   result, add the missing rule to the skill. That correction is now permanent.
5. **Repeat until you trust it.** Two or three rounds is normal. Mediocre first
   output means the skill is missing a sentence, not that AI "doesn't work."

## Where to learn more

- **The test:** if a smart new hire could follow your skill, so can the AI. If
  they couldn't, the skill (not the hire) needs another sentence.
