# Teach the AI Your Job

*Handout from Marie's session at Victoria Tech Week. You can use everything on
this page whether or not you were in the room.*

## The idea, in two sentences

Most of us ask AI to do a task, then spend real time cleaning up what it
hands back. Write down, once, in plain English, how you actually do that
task (the way you'd brief a new hire) and hand the AI that file instead of a
one-off prompt: that's called a skill, and it runs the same way every time.

## Why bother

- Regular AI use at work rose 13% in 2025, to 45% of workers. (ManpowerGroup
  Global Talent Barometer 2026, about fourteen thousand workers in 19
  countries, published January 2026.)
- Confidence in using technology fell 18% the same year, the first drop in
  three years. That's workers' confidence in their own ability with
  technology, not trust in AI.
- For every ten hours AI saves someone at work, nearly four go back into
  correcting, clarifying or rewriting what it made. (Workday, "Beyond
  Productivity: Measuring the Real Value of AI," 14 January 2026, 3,200
  full-time employees who use AI at work.)

A skill is how you stop paying that second number. You explain the job
once, not every morning.

## Build your first skill this week

1. **Pick your most-repeated task.** Not the hardest one you own, the one
   you'll do again next week: the status update, the meeting notes, the
   same-five-questions email.
2. **Write it down, plain English, one page, five parts:**
   - *The job:* one sentence, what this skill is for.
   - *Ask first:* what the AI needs before it starts (audience, deadline,
     inputs).
   - *The steps:* how you actually do the task, in order.
   - *What good looks like:* tone, length, format, an example if you have
     one.
   - *Never:* the mistakes and no-go zones, things it must not claim, send,
     or invent.
3. **Run it on something real.** Yesterday's meeting, last week's numbers.
   Not a made-up example.
4. **When it gets something wrong, fix the skill, not that one output.**
   Add the missing sentence to the file. The fix is now permanent, and it
   applies the next time too.
5. **Repeat until you trust it.** Two or three rounds is normal. A weak
   first draft means the skill is missing a sentence, not that AI doesn't
   work for this.

A simple test: if a smart new hire could follow your written instructions,
the AI can too. If they'd get stuck, that's the sentence your skill is
missing, not a reason to give up.

## One format, any AI

A skill is a plain-English file, not code. Since December 2025 it's an open
standard, so the same approach works in Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, or
whatever your company already uses. You're learning to teach an AI, not to
operate one particular product.
